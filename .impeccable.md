## Design Context

### Users and use case

FPFA serves policy professionals, researchers, international-relations students, and interested readers who need a fast, reliable view of current foreign-policy analysis. Most sessions are short and reading-focused, on phones as well as desktop screens.

### Brand personality

Measured, informed, and unobtrusive. The interface should feel editorial and trustworthy, never playful, promotional, or theatrical.

### Aesthetic direction

Use a light editorial reader with a restrained layered-card motif. The active article is the clear reading surface; neighbouring cards suggest chronology without competing with it. Typography, spacing, and contrast carry the design. Avoid generic dashboard grids, excessive gradients, decorative shadows, and animation without informational value. Preserve the existing blue accent while meeting WCAG AA contrast.

### Design principles

1. Put the latest verified article first and make chronology explicit.
2. Keep content primary: title, source, date, thesis, summary, and evidence must scan cleanly.
3. Make every state explicit: loading, empty, stale cache, offline, and failure.
4. Support keyboard, screen readers, reduced motion, and touch targets of at least 44 pixels.
5. Adapt from 320-pixel phones to wide desktop screens without clipping or fixed-layout assumptions.
6. Prefer small payloads, stable rendering, and reversible interaction over visual spectacle.
